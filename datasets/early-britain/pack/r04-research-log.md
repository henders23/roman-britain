## Notes from research
- group a: No duplicates with r03: none of these events or candidate_ids are in the pack. Hadrian's Wall already has an exclude row in r03 (eb:hadrians-wall), so no new exclude row is added. Dates: day-precision dates are written 211-02-04 and 306-07-25 (src/data/time.ts accepts 1–4 digit years). All date_starts checked against phases: 122 high-roman, 211 high-roman, 286 late-roman (285–409), 306 late-roman, 407 late-roman. Carausius: entered as range 286–287; most recent work (Casey) favours 286 but 287 has supporters. If the editor wants a single year, use 286 circa. Severus: one row only, for the death at York; the 208–211 campaigns are in the note and narrative. A separate conflict-kind row for the campaigns could be an area over eastern Scotland, but the marching-camp dating is too contested to place confidently, so it is not offered. The 4 February date derives from Dio's age/reign figures. RIB 1051: its date and purpose (Wall completion monument vs. victory monument) are debated; it is cited as secondary, not as evidence of the visit itself. Olympiodorus fragment number: I cited fr. 13 (Blockley) as requested by the task, but one search result assigns the British usurpers to fr. 12 in a different numbering; please check against Blockley. Kind: all five are political; Carausius and Constantine III could arguably be conflict (revolt), but the seizures of power themselves are better described as political. Images skipped as instructed.
- group b: Mons Graupius: date given as range 83–84 (governorship start 77 or 78). Halo centre (57.10 N, 2.45 W) with 45 km radius covers Bennachie/Durno, Raedykes and Kempstone Hill and reaches near Duns Dish; Moray and more southerly candidates fall outside. No independent second primary source exists. Inchtuthil: used circa 83 with date_end 87 (built c. 82–83, lasted to 86/87) rather than range, since the row describes a site that existed over the span; switch to range 82–87 if preferred. The nail hoard could be a merge row later. Bloomberg: the collection is dated as a 50–80 range; if the editor prefers to pin the dated document, WT44 could carry date_start 0057-01-08 with day precision, but the validator's handling of a 2-digit year in YYYY-MM-DD form is untested, so I did not do this. Caerleon: kind fort (a military amphitheatre of the fortress). Added an exclude row for the Arthurian Round Table legend. Silchester ogham: phase after-rome with window 410–650, but Fulford et al. 2000 argue for a date around or slightly before 410 (well closure c. 350–425). If their view is followed the row would need late-roman with a window starting c. 350–400. Pin is on Insula IX, approximate. Reading Museum accession number not found. No images (images part skipped as instructed).
- group c: - Wearmouth and Jarrow are TWO rows (wearmouth-foundation-674, jarrow-dedication-685): the houses are ~10 km apart and dated by different evidence (Bede's narrative vs a dated inscription). Jarrow is dated by the dedication stone (685-04-23, precision day); the 681/682 foundation is described in its narrative. Kind for Jarrow is religious (a church dedication), though the evidence is an inscription; editor could choose `text` instead.
- Columba: precision `circa` on the traditional 563; Bede gives 565. Coordinates of Iona Abbey (-6.392, 56.334) are from general knowledge, not a confirmed grid reference.
- Aidan: Bede gives no year; 635 is inferred (see note). HE 3.17 locator not confirmed online.
- Athelney: the brief cited Asser 53; c. 53 covers Alfred's retreat into the Somerset fens, c. 55 the building of the fortress after Easter (Keynes and Lapidge numbering); I cite both. The existing Edington row cites Asser c. 56, consistent with this. Coordinates are from the scheduled monument's grid ref (51°03′33″N 2°56′04″W).
- Cakes exclude row: the Vita S. Neoti is dated late 10th century in the search results; some scholars (Lapidge) prefer early 11th, so the reason says 'late 10th or early 11th'.
- Alfred Jewel: accession AN1836 p.135.371 confirmed. Certainty set to medium because the 871–899 window depends on identifying the inscribed Ælfred with the king. Drawn as an area (4 km halo on North Petherton) because the find-spot is known only as Newton Park in the parish; I could not confirm Newton Park's exact location, so the halo is centred on North Petherton village. Kind hoard (significant find) as instructed.
- Wallingford: list entry 1006293 confirmed via Gatehouse (not the HE page itself). Window 878–919 is my construction (after Edington to the latest usual date of the Burghal Hidage); the editor may prefer a narrower 880–919. Note the HE entry 1006088 that appeared in searches is Tamworth, not Wallingford.
- images: omitted as instructed.
