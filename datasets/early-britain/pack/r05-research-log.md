## Notes from research
- group a: Phases: Uley is placed in early-roman because the stone temple dates to the early 2nd century (window 100-150); if the editor prefers to anchor the card on the curse tablets (mostly 2nd-4th century) it could move to high-roman with a later window. Lydney's window 250-370 deliberately spans Casey and Hoffmann's later-3rd-century start and Wheeler's 'soon after 364-7'; phase high-roman follows date_start. Carrawburgh is pinned at the fort per the brief (location_certainty approximate); Hadrian's Wall already has an exclude row (eb:hadrians-wall), so no new linear-monument row was added. The London Mithraeum pin (-0.091, 51.512) is a few metres from the existing Bloomberg tablets pin (-0.091, 51.513); both are genuinely on the same site. Not confirmed online: the report-series number of Shepherd 1998 (EH Archaeological Report 12), the Research Report number of Wheeler 1932 (9), and the Archaeologia Aeliana volume (29) for Richmond and Gillam 1951. Carrawburgh phase dates (e.g. a rebuild c. 222, destruction c. 296-7 or c. 330/350) vary between popular sources and were not used in the row. The number of Uley tablets varies by source (c. 87 readable, over 140 items with blanks and fragments); the card says 'well over a hundred'. Images: Uley has no confirmed Commons image. The Notitia page is used for the 367 conspiracy card as an honest manuscript illustration of the coastal command; it would also suit the Portchester card, which instead uses a photograph of the actual Roman walls. A Portable Antiquities Scheme coin of Valentinian I (FindID 428622) was considered for the conspiracy, but its CC licence version could not be confirmed.
- group b: Group r05-b: Picts, Scots and Britons, and Wales after Rome. No candidate_id or canonical_id duplicates r04 (checked; nearest existing row is columba-iona-563). Kinds: Dunadd, Burghead and Dinas Powys are all given kind 'fort' for consistency; Dinas Powys could reasonably be 'villa' (high-status residence) instead. Aberlemno is 'religious' (churchyard cross-slabs); the roadside Serpent Stone is a non-Christian symbol stone, so the editor may prefer a different kind. Dates: Burghead's ramparts may have been begun in the 3rd–4th century (late-roman phase), but the row is placed in after-rome with date_start 500 circa for the radiocarbon-attested main occupation (from at least the 6th century) — the editor may want date_start 550. Dunadd is 500–850 circa. Dinas Powys 450–680 circa follows the 2023 Antiquity chronology. Dun Nechtain: area halo of 50 km centred midway between Dunnichen (c. 56.628 N, 2.806 W) and Dunachton (c. 57.13 N, 3.90 W, estimated, not confirmed online); the centre falls in the hills of upper Glen Shee/Atholl, which is NOT a candidate site — the uncertainty note says so. An alternative is to draw the halo at Dunnichen with a note. Hywel Dda: drawn as a 5 km halo at Whitland, certainty low; the codification itself may be legend, so the editor may prefer to treat it as an excluded legend plus an included row for Peniarth 28 (which is in Aberystwyth). Locators not confirmed online: exact Annals of Ulster entry numbers (683, 686=685, 736, 870, 871); authors/volume of the 1980 PSAS Burghead article (Edwards and Ralston, PSAS 110); Studia Celtica 47 for Seaman 2013; Antiquaries Journal 89 (2009) for Edwards; Edwards Corpus vol. 3 number for the pillar. Coordinates for Aberlemno churchyard, Dumbarton Rock and Whitland are from general knowledge. Images: Dunadd photo is the general hill view (a footprint close-up 'Dunadd Fort 20080427 - footprint.jpg' was mentioned in snippets but its page title never appeared in results, so not used). Girtin's Dumbarton watercolour: file title confirmed, licence inferred from the artist's death in 1802 (Google Art Project scans are tagged PD-Art). No picture for Dun Nechtain or Dinas Powys.
- group c: Phases: Lichfield Angel (c. 800) is placed in norse-age, as the brief requires; the other five events fall in kingdoms. The Offa dinar date is a 774–796 window; the British Museum gives the model as AH 157 (773–774), while the Commons file description says AH 156. I followed the museum.
BM 1913,1213.1: a search for the number returned the BM Images and Google Arts & Culture pages for the coin, but the BM collection-database page itself did not appear. Please check it.
Offa dinar geometry: no mint or findspot is known (it was recorded in Rome in 1841), so the row uses a 60 km halo over London and Canterbury, Offa's main mints. An editor might prefer a larger halo over southern England.
Brixworth: the list entry still says C7. The row gives a 675–800 window with certainty medium, because Fernie's 800–860 dating would put the church in norse-age. If the editor prefers the radiocarbon date alone, use c. 775–800.
Ipswich: I made one row for the town and Ipswich ware together. A separate row for the Ipswich ware industry could be added, but it would duplicate the place pin. The start date of 650 is approximate; some sources put the landing place as early as c. 625.
Hamwic: I did not add merges for the Six Dials or other excavations, since they are cited as primary locators. Willibald's 'Hamwih' and ASC 840 are mentioned in the narrative only.
Exclusion: eb:offa-convert-to-islam is a modern popular claim and is excluded as legend. Offa's Dyke is already excluded in r04 (eb:offas-dyke), so it is not repeated here.
