# Emperors in Britain
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

Britain was a distant province, yet emperors came here, died here and were made here. This journey follows them across four centuries. Notice what kind of source puts each emperor in Britain: a later biography, a historian writing long after, an inscription, or coins.

## Stop: camulodunum-surrender-43
Claudius came in person for the capture of Camulodunum and stayed only days. His visit was a piece of theatre for Rome, commemorated on a triumphal arch whose inscription survives in fragments.

## Stop: hadrian-visit-britain-122
Hadrian's visit rests mainly on one line in a much later imperial biography, which says he came to put many things right and to build a wall to separate Romans from barbarians. A military diploma dated that summer helps fix the year. Where he went is not recorded, so the map draws a broad region.

## Stop: severus-death-york-211
Septimius Severus spent his last years campaigning in the far north and died at York in February 211. The campaigns left marching camps that are hard to date; his death is fixed by the historians of the period.

## Stop: carausius-usurpation-286
Carausius, commander of the Channel fleet, made himself emperor and ruled Britain as a breakaway state for seven years. His own coins, with their bold slogans, are among the best evidence for how he wanted to be seen.

## Stop: constantine-proclaimed-york-306
When the emperor Constantius died at York, his army proclaimed his son Constantine. The sources disagree on the title he was first given, and not all of them say where it happened.

## Stop: constantine-iii-proclaimed-407
The last emperor made in Britain was a soldier named Constantine, raised by the army in 407. He took troops to Gaul and did not return. Within a few years Roman government in Britain had ended.
