# Christianity in Roman Britain
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: thematic

No written account tells us how Christianity spread in Roman Britain. What survives are objects and buildings, most of them from the fourth century, and each has to be interpreted. This journey visits four of them and asks, at each stop, how we know the object is Christian at all. The first three are dated only to the fourth century, so they are ordered by argument rather than by date.

## Stop: water-newton-treasure
The clearest case. Several of these silver vessels and plaques carry the chi-rho monogram and inscriptions that are plainly Christian, which makes the hoard some of the earliest Christian church plate known from anywhere in the Roman empire.

## Stop: hinton-st-mary-mosaic
Here the argument is harder. The mosaic's central bust has a chi-rho behind it and is usually read as Christ, which would make it one of the earliest images of him in Britain. Some scholars argue it could be an emperor. The pack keeps that as interpretation, not fact.

## Stop: mildenhall-treasure
A great silver treasure with mainly pagan imagery, but a few spoons carry Christian symbols. Its owners' beliefs are not straightforward, and the circumstances of its discovery in the 1940s have themselves been questioned.

## Stop: lullingstone-house-church
At Lullingstone, a room in a villa was painted in the later fourth century with chi-rho monograms and figures at prayer: a Christian space inside a private house, found by excavation and pieced back together from fallen plaster.
