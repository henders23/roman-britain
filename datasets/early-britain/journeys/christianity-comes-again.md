# Christianity comes again
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: gods-saints-and-the-church

Christians were meeting in a villa in Kent in the fourth century. So why did missionaries have to come to Britain from Iona and from Rome two centuries later? This journey follows Christianity from Roman Britain to the meeting at Whitby where two missions settled their differences.

## Stop: lullingstone-house-church
In the later fourth century an upper room in the villa at Lullingstone was painted with figures praying and chi-rho monograms. It is evidence of Christian worship in a wealthy Roman household.

## Stop: columba-iona-563
Columba, an Irish abbot, crossed to Britain and founded a monastery on Iona, by tradition in 563. Iona became the mother house of churches across Ireland and northern Britain, and its monks would carry the faith further.

## Stop: augustine-canterbury-597
In 597 monks sent from Rome by Pope Gregory and led by Augustine arrived in Kent. King Æthelberht allowed them to settle in Canterbury. A second mission, from a different tradition, had begun in the south.

## Stop: aidan-lindisfarne-635
King Oswald of Northumbria, who had spent his exile among the monks of Iona, asked them for a bishop. Aidan came and was given the island of Lindisfarne. The Iona mission now reached the English.

## Stop: synod-of-whitby-664
The two traditions dated Easter differently. In 664 King Oswiu held a meeting at Whitby and decided for the Roman method. Bede's account of it is vivid, but it was written two generations later.

## Closing
The Christianity of Roman Britain survived among the Britons, but the English kingdoms had to be converted by missions from Iona and from Rome. At Whitby the two met, and Northumbria chose Rome's way. The written story comes almost entirely from Bede, writing in that Roman tradition.
