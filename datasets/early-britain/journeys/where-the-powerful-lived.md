# Where the powerful lived
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: towns-trade-and-everyday-life

How did the wealthy show their power? This journey visits five homes of the powerful, from a Roman palace to an early English royal settlement, and asks what changed and what stayed the same once Rome had gone.

## Stop: fishbourne-palace
Near Chichester, a vast courtyard residence was built in the later first century, with mosaics, painted walls and a formal garden. Who lived there is argued: perhaps a British king who ruled as Rome's ally, perhaps a Roman official.

## Stop: hinton-st-mary-mosaic
In a villa building in Dorset, a fourth-century mosaic floor shows a man's head before a chi-rho, usually read as Christ. Wealthy owners displayed their beliefs, as well as their taste, on their floors.

## Stop: lullingstone-house-church
At Lullingstone in Kent, the owners of a villa had an upper room painted with figures at prayer and chi-rho monograms in the later fourth century. A great house could contain its own place of worship.

## Stop: dinas-powys-hillfort
After Rome, a wealthy household near Cardiff lived inside a small hillfort and imported wine, pottery and glass from the Mediterranean and Gaul. Power had moved from villas to defended hilltops.

## Stop: rendlesham-royal-settlement
Bede names Rendlesham in Suffolk as a royal settlement of the East Anglian kings, and fieldwork since 2008 has found a large and wealthy site there. It is a new kind of power, built around a king and his hall.

## Closing
Across six centuries the powerful went on displaying wealth, imported goods and belief in their homes. What changed was the setting: from Roman palaces and villas to defended hilltops and royal halls. The ground shows the homes far better than any written source shows the people in them.
