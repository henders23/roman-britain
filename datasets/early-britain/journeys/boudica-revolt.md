# Boudica's revolt
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

In AD 60 or 61 the Iceni and their neighbours rose against Roman rule. Almost everything we know about the course of the revolt comes from two Roman historians, Tacitus and Cassius Dio, writing decades or more later. The archaeology is different evidence: layers of burnt debris in three towns. Follow the revolt, and keep asking which kind of evidence each stop rests on. The camera moves between places, but no route is drawn, because nobody knows the rebels' route.

## Stop: mona-attack-60
Tacitus says the governor, Suetonius Paulinus, was far away on Anglesey, attacking a centre of resistance and of the druids, when the revolt broke out. The atlas draws the island as a region because the sources give no more precise place.

## Stop: camulodunum-destruction-60
The colony at Colchester, with its temple to the deified Claudius, was the first target. Tacitus describes its fall; excavations across the town have found a thick burnt layer of about this date. The two kinds of evidence agree, but they are not the same thing.

## Stop: londinium-destruction-60
London was a trading town without walls. Tacitus says Paulinus judged it could not be held and left it to the rebels. A burnt horizon of this period runs under the modern City.

## Stop: verulamium-destruction-60
The rebels next took Verulamium, near modern St Albans. Excavation found burnt shops of this date. Tacitus's figures for the dead in the three towns are huge, and are best read as a Roman writer's rhetoric rather than a count.

## Stop: boudica-final-battle-60
The revolt ended in a single battle that the Romans won. No ancient writer says where it was fought, and no battlefield has been found, so the map draws a broad region rather than inventing a pin. Compare the certainty here with the burnt towns.
