# St Cuthbert's community on the move
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: gods-saints-and-the-church

For more than three centuries one community kept its identity: the monks of Lindisfarne, who later guarded St Cuthbert's body, carried it from the island and settled at last at Durham. This journey begins with the founding of their monastery. Much of the later story is told by the community's own histories, which are sometimes more concerned with the saint's authority than with exact dates.

## Stop: aidan-lindisfarne-635
King Oswald of Northumbria invited Aidan, a monk of Iona, to found a monastery on Lindisfarne. Cuthbert would later be its bishop, and after his death in 687 his tomb made the island a place of pilgrimage.

## Stop: lindisfarne-gospels
The community's great book was made in the early eighth century. A note added in the tenth century says it was written by Eadfrith, bishop of Lindisfarne. By tradition the book went with the community on its travels.

## Stop: lindisfarne-raid-793
Lindisfarne, Cuthbert's monastery, was the first major English church that Viking raiders are recorded to have attacked. News of it shocked scholars as far away as Charlemagne's court.

## Stop: cuthbert-community-leaves-lindisfarne-875
With Viking armies in the north, the community left the island in 875, carrying Cuthbert's body. The date and the story come from the community's own later histories.

## Stop: cuthbert-community-chester-le-street-883
After some years of wandering, the community settled at Chester-le-Street, where it stayed for more than a century.

## Stop: cuthbert-community-durham-995
In 995 the community moved to Durham, where Cuthbert's shrine still lies. A later story says they found the place by following a woman looking for her cow; the pack keeps that tale off the map.

## Closing
The community survived raids, years of wandering and two moves by keeping two things: Cuthbert's body and its own story about him. That story is also the main source for its journey, so the dates are the community's own. Durham Cathedral, built over the shrine, is the end of the road.
