# Roman Britain in six stops
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: start-here

In AD 43 British kings submitted to a Roman emperor who had come to see Colchester taken. Less than four centuries later, Britain was no longer governed from Rome. This short journey is a first look at the whole Roman period: how it began, how it was held, and how it ended. Ask at each stop how we know.

## Stop: camulodunum-surrender-43
The emperor Claudius came to Britain for only a few days to see Camulodunum taken and to receive the submission of British rulers. His arch in Rome records kings who surrendered to him; the number, probably eleven, is partly restored. Rome's rule in Britain begins with a ceremony as much as a battle.

## Stop: hadrian-visit-britain-122
Eighty years on, the emperor Hadrian visited the province. His biography links the visit to the building of a wall across the north. Where he went in Britain is not recorded, so the map draws a region rather than a pin.

## Stop: barbarian-conspiracy-367
In 367 Picts, Scots and Attacotti attacked Britain while Franks and Saxons raided Gaul. The historian Ammianus calls it a conspiracy of barbarians. Whether the attacks were really planned together is debated, but the province was badly shaken.

## Stop: constantine-iii-proclaimed-407
In 407 the army in Britain made a soldier named Constantine emperor. He took the province's mobile troops to Gaul to fight for the western empire. They did not come back.

## Stop: hoxne-hoard
Some time after 408 someone in Suffolk buried a chest of gold and silver with almost fifteen thousand coins. Its latest coins, with finds from many other sites, show that new money had almost stopped reaching Britain. The owners never came back for it.

## Stop: rescript-of-honorius-410
A Greek historian writing a century later says that in 410 the emperor Honorius told the cities of Britain to look to their own defence. It is the famous end date, but it rests on one sentence that may not even refer to Britain.

## Closing
Roman Britain began with an emperor's few days at Colchester and ended without any single moment the evidence can fix. Emperors came and went, the frontier was built and defended, and in the end the army left. Each of the other journeys looks more closely at part of this story.
