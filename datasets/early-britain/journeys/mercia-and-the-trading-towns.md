# Mercia and the trading towns
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

In the seventh and eighth centuries new trading towns grew up on rivers and coasts, and the kingdom of Mercia became the strongest power in southern Britain. This journey moves between the towns, where archaeology has found the evidence of everyday trade, and the treasures and churches of Mercian kings.

## Stop: lundenwic
Not inside the old Roman walls but along the Strand, Saxon London grew into a busy trading town. Bede called it a market for many peoples; excavation under Covent Garden found its streets, houses and workshops.

## Stop: gipeswic-ipswich
Ipswich was another early trading town, and its potters made Ipswich ware, the first pottery made on a wheel in England since Roman times. Finds of it across the east of England map where Ipswich traded.

## Stop: staffordshire-hoard
In the heart of Mercia, someone buried thousands of gold and garnet fittings stripped from weapons. It shows the wealth of war that underpinned Mercian power.

## Stop: brixworth-all-saints
Brixworth is one of the largest buildings to survive from Anglo-Saxon England, built partly with reused Roman brick. Its date is argued over, but it shows the ambition of the Mercian church.

## Stop: hamwic
The West Saxons had their own trading town at Hamwic, on the site of Southampton, laid out with gravelled streets and crowded with craftworkers.

## Stop: offa-gold-dinar
Offa of Mercia had a gold coin made that copies an Islamic dinar, Arabic writing and all, with his own name added. It is evidence of how far trade and ideas travelled; why he had it made is unknown.

## Stop: lichfield-angel
Under the floor of Lichfield Cathedral, excavators found a carved and painted angel of about 800, probably from a shrine at the heart of Mercia's chief church.

## Stop: repton-winter-camp-873
Mercian power ended with the Viking great army, which wintered at Repton, a royal and religious centre of Mercia, and drove out its king.
