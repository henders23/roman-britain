# Still arguing: five debates
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: how-do-we-know

Historians and archaeologists still disagree about a great deal in this period. This journey visits five arguments that are not settled: a river, a face in a mosaic, a battle site, a jewel's purpose and a famous battle. At each stop, ask what evidence would settle it.

## Stop: river-battle-43
Which river did the Roman army fight across in AD 43? Dio does not say. The Medway is the usual answer, but it is an identification made by modern historians, not by the source.

## Stop: hinton-st-mary-mosaic
Is the face in the centre of this fourth-century mosaic Christ? It has a chi-rho behind it, and most scholars think so, which would make it one of the earliest images of Christ in Britain. Some argue it shows an emperor.

## Stop: dun-nechtain-685
Where did the Picts defeat Northumbria in 685? The annals name Dún Nechtain. Dunnichen in Angus has long been the favourite, but Dunachton in Badenoch has strong support.

## Stop: alfred-jewel
What was the Alfred Jewel for, and which Alfred ordered it? It is usually read as the handle of a pointer for following a text, perhaps one sent with the books King Alfred gave his bishops. Both ideas are interpretation.

## Stop: battle-of-brunanburh-937
Where was Brunanburh fought? Many sites have been proposed. Bromborough on the Wirral is the leading candidate, and finds of tenth-century weapon fragments nearby have been reported, but nothing has settled it.

## Closing
Each of these debates survives because the evidence stops just short of an answer: a source that names no river, a face without a label, a place-name that fits two sites, an object without its owner. The atlas keeps these as interpretation rather than choosing a winner.
