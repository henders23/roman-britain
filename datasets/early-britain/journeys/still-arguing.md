# Still arguing: five debates
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: how-do-we-know

Historians and archaeologists still disagree about a great deal in this period. This journey visits five arguments that are not settled: a river, a face in a mosaic, a battle site, a jewel's purpose and a famous battle. At each stop, ask what evidence would settle it.

## Stop: river-battle-43
Which river did the Roman army fight across in AD 43? Dio does not say. Writing some 170 years later, he is the only source: he describes Britons camped on the far bank, troops trained to swim in armour crossing to attack the chariot horses, and fighting that went into a second day before Hosidius Geta won it. The Medway is the usual answer, but it is an identification made by modern historians, not by the source. It fits a landing at Richborough and a march west towards the Thames, while scholars who favour a landing near Chichester have proposed other rivers. No battlefield has been found, so the map shows a halo over the lower Medway.

## Stop: hinton-st-mary-mosaic
Is the face in the centre of this fourth-century mosaic Christ? Found in Dorset in 1963 and lifted to the British Museum in 1965, the floor covered two connected rooms of a villa building. The clean-shaven bust is set before a chi-rho flanked by pomegranates, and the smaller room shows Bellerophon on Pegasus killing the Chimaera. Most scholars think the figure is Christ, which would make it one of the earliest images of Christ in Britain. Some argue it shows an emperor, since the chi-rho was also used on imperial coins, and how far pagan and Christian themes were meant to be read together is argued too. Nothing on the floor names the figure.

## Stop: dun-nechtain-685
Where did the Picts defeat Northumbria in 685? On 20 May that year a Pictish army under Bridei son of Beli destroyed the Northumbrian army and killed King Ecgfrith. The annals name the place as Dún Nechtain, while Bede says the Picts feigned flight and drew Ecgfrith into narrow places among inaccessible mountains. Dunnichen in Angus has long been the favourite, but Dunachton in Badenoch has strong support. Both names derive from Dún Nechtain, and in 2006 Alex Woolf argued that Dunachton's mountain passes fit Bede better. The answer matters beyond the battle, because it bears on where the Pictish kingdom of Fortriu lay.

## Stop: alfred-jewel
What was the Alfred Jewel for, and which Alfred ordered it? It was ploughed up in 1693 at North Petherton in Somerset, a few miles from Athelney, and is now in the Ashmolean Museum. Its gold frame carries the openwork Old English words 'Alfred ordered me to be made', around a rock crystal over an enamel figure, and it ends in an animal head holding a small socket. The inscription names an Ælfred but not a king, so the link with King Alfred rests on the name, the find-spot and the style of the goldwork. It is usually read as the handle of a pointer for following a text, perhaps one of the aestels Alfred said he sent with copies of his translation of Gregory's Pastoral Care. Both ideas are interpretation.

## Stop: battle-of-brunanburh-937
Where was Brunanburh fought? In 937 Æthelstan and his brother Edmund defeated Olaf of Dublin, Constantine of Alba and the Strathclyde Britons there, and the Chronicle celebrates the victory in a poem. The poem says the beaten Norse fled by ship over Dingesmere to Dublin, which suggests a western coast, but no source places the battle clearly. Many sites have been proposed, among them Burnswark in Dumfriesshire and Lanchester in County Durham. Bromborough on the Wirral is the leading candidate, because its name is widely thought to derive from Brunanburh, and finds of tenth-century weapon fragments nearby have been reported. They are not yet fully published and their link to the battle is interpretation, so nothing has settled it.

## Closing
Each of these debates survives because the evidence stops just short of an answer: a source that names no river, a face without a label, a place-name that fits two sites, an object without its owner. The atlas keeps these as interpretation rather than choosing a winner.
