# Burials, treasure and early English kings
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

Kings in the sixth and seventh centuries left little writing, but some were buried, or buried their wealth, in ways that still astonish. This journey visits four places and asks how far we can move from what was found to who these people were.

## Stop: prittlewell-burial
A richly furnished chamber burial in Essex, dated by radiocarbon and coins to about 580–605. Its goods include imports from the Mediterranean and small gold-foil crosses. Who was buried is unknown; a member of the East Saxon royal family has been suggested.

## Stop: sutton-hoo-mound1
A ship dragged up from the river and buried under a mound, with a chamber of treasure. The identification with King Rædwald of East Anglia is widely proposed but not proven, which is why the pack keeps it as an interpretation.

## Stop: staffordshire-hoard
Thousands of fragments of gold and garnet fittings stripped from weapons and war-gear, buried in Mercia in the later seventh century. Why it was buried, and whose it was, remain open questions.

## Stop: rendlesham-royal-settlement
Bede names Rendlesham as an East Anglian royal residence, and survey has found a large settlement of high status nearby. Here, unusually, a written source and the ground meet, although the map draws the site as a small region rather than a precise point.
