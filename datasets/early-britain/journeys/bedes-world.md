# Bede's world
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: gods-saints-and-the-church

Bede entered the monastery at Wearmouth as a child and spent his life at its twin house at Jarrow. This journey follows the half-century he lived through: the founding of his monasteries, a battle that shook Northumbria, a great gospel book, and his own history. Much of what we know of these years, we know because he wrote it down.

## Stop: wearmouth-foundation-674
In 674 Benedict Biscop founded a monastery at the mouth of the Wear, on land given by King Ecgfrith, and built its church in stone with craftsmen from Gaul. Bede was given to this monastery as a boy.

## Stop: jarrow-dedication-685
A stone inscription in St Paul's church at Jarrow records that it was dedicated on 23 April 685. It is one of the few dates of this age written at the time rather than by a later historian.

## Stop: dun-nechtain-685
A month later, on 20 May 685, a Pictish army destroyed the army of the same King Ecgfrith at Dún Nechtain, and the king was killed. Northumbria's power over the Picts collapsed. Where the battle was fought is disputed.

## Stop: lindisfarne-gospels
In the early eighth century the Lindisfarne Gospels were made in Northumbria, combining Irish, Anglo-Saxon and Mediterranean styles. They show the same world of learning and craft that produced Bede.

## Stop: bede-historia-731
In 731 Bede finished his Ecclesiastical History of the English People at Jarrow. It is the main written source for the conversion of the English, and for every earlier stop on this journey.

## Closing
Bede's world was small in space, two monasteries a few miles apart, but it reached Rome, Gaul and Ireland through books, builders and teachers. Even the battle at Dún Nechtain is known mainly through Bede's account. When we read this period, we often read it through his eyes.
