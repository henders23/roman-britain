# How did Roman Britain end?
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

Textbooks often give 410 as the end of Roman Britain. This journey tests that date against the evidence. Notice how each stop is a different kind of source, and how uncertain each one is.

## Stop: hoxne-hoard
Someone buried this treasure of gold, silver and coins in Suffolk after about 408. Its latest coins show when the flow of new money into Britain stopped, which is some of the strongest evidence for the end of Roman government. Why the owners never came back is unknown.

## Stop: rescript-of-honorius-410
The famous date rests on one sentence by a Greek historian writing about a century later, reporting that the emperor told the cities of Britain to defend themselves. Some scholars think the sentence was about Bruttium in Italy. The map draws it as a faint region with low certainty.

## Stop: tintagel-trading-site
In the century after Rome, Tintagel was receiving wine and oil from the Mediterranean. Rome's government had gone, but the west of Britain was still connected to the Roman world by trade. The legend of Arthur at Tintagel is kept off the map; the imported pottery is the evidence.
