# Vikings in the north
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Collection: vikings-and-england

After the raid on Lindisfarne in 793, what did Scandinavians do in northern Britain: raid, trade or settle? This journey follows them for nearly three centuries, from a monastery on an island to a Norwegian king's last battle near York.

## Stop: lindisfarne-raid-793
In 793 raiders from the sea attacked the monastery on Lindisfarne. It is the first Viking raid on a major English church that the sources describe in detail.

## Stop: dumbarton-siege-870
In 870 Scandinavian kings from Dublin besieged Alt Clut, the rock fortress of the Britons on the Clyde, for four months and destroyed it. Vikings in Ireland were now reaching deep into northern Britain.

## Stop: cuthbert-community-leaves-lindisfarne-875
Five years later, as a Viking army moved north, the community of St Cuthbert left Lindisfarne with the saint's body. Raiding had become conquest.

## Stop: cuerdale-hoard
Around 905–910 a chest of about 40 kilograms of silver was buried at a crossing of the Ribble in Lancashire. Its coins come from Viking York, East Anglia, the Frankish lands and the Islamic world.

## Stop: coppergate-tenements
In York, excavation at Coppergate found tenth-century houses and workshops preserved in wet ground. The Vikings who had come as raiders were now townspeople, craftsmen and traders.

## Stop: battle-of-stamford-bridge-1066
In September 1066 the Norwegian king Harald Hardrada invaded and was killed by King Harold at Stamford Bridge, near York. It was the last great Norwegian invasion of England.

## Closing
In the north, Vikings came first as raiders, then as armies and kings, and then as settlers and traders whose silver and streets archaeologists still find. The story ends where it was strongest, near York, with a Norwegian king's defeat. Three days later another descendant of Scandinavians, William of Normandy, landed in the south.
