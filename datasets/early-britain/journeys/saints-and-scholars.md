# Saints and scholars of the north
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: chronological

In the seventh century the north of Britain became one of the great centres of learning in Europe. This journey follows the monks who made it so, from Iona to Jarrow, and ends with the raid that shook their world. Much of the story is told by Bede, who belonged to it.

## Stop: columba-iona-563
Columba, an Irish monk, founded a monastery on Iona. The traditional date comes from Irish annals; Bede gives a slightly different year. Radiocarbon dates from the island fit the later sixth century.

## Stop: aidan-lindisfarne-635
King Oswald of Northumbria, who had been in exile among the Iona monks, invited Aidan to found a monastery on Lindisfarne. Bede gives no year, so 635 is worked out from his account.

## Stop: synod-of-whitby-664
At Whitby the Northumbrian church chose Roman over Irish ways of dating Easter. Bede's account is vivid, and written two generations later.

## Stop: wearmouth-foundation-674
Benedict Biscop, who had travelled to Rome many times, founded the monastery at Wearmouth and filled it with books, glass and paintings from the continent.

## Stop: jarrow-dedication-685
Its twin house at Jarrow was dedicated on a day recorded on a stone that still survives in the church. Bede entered the community as a child.

## Stop: lindisfarne-gospels
The Lindisfarne Gospels were made in the early eighth century, combining Irish, Anglo-Saxon and Mediterranean styles.

## Stop: bede-historia-731
At Jarrow, Bede finished his Ecclesiastical History. It is the main reason this journey can be told at all.

## Stop: lindisfarne-raid-793
Sixty years later, raiders struck Lindisfarne. To the scholars of the north it seemed the end of an age.
