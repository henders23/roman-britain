# Alfred's kingdom
By: UK Atlas (atlas synthesis)
Order: thematic

In 878 King Alfred of Wessex was nearly defeated. Within a generation his family ruled much of England. This journey follows the recovery and the kingdom he built, and keeps the famous legend about the cakes off the map. The stops follow the story rather than strict date order, since the Alfred Jewel can only be dated to his reign as a whole.

## Stop: alfred-athelney-878
After a surprise attack early in 878, Alfred retreated into the Somerset marshes and built a fortification at Athelney. The Chronicle and his biographer Asser tell the story; the tale of the burnt cakes appears only much later, so the pack excludes it.

## Stop: battle-of-edington-878
From Athelney Alfred gathered an army and defeated the Viking leader Guthrum at Ethandun, usually identified as Edington. Guthrum accepted baptism and a peace followed.

## Stop: alfred-jewel
Found in 1693 a few miles from Athelney, the jewel carries the words 'Alfred ordered me to be made'. It was probably the handle of a pointer for following a text, perhaps one of the books Alfred had sent to his bishops. That use, and the identity of the Alfred named, are interpretation.

## Stop: wallingford-burh
To defend Wessex, Alfred and his successors built a network of fortified towns, or burhs, listed in a document called the Burghal Hidage. Wallingford's earth ramparts still survive.

## Stop: battle-of-brunanburh-937
Alfred's grandson Athelstan defeated a coalition of northern kings at Brunanburh, a victory celebrated as the moment the English kingdom held. From the marshes of Athelney to Brunanburh is less than sixty years.
