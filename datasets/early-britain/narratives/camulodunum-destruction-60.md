## Summary
The first target of Boudica's revolt was the Roman colony at Camulodunum (Colchester), which the rebels destroyed. The last defenders held out in the Temple of Claudius for two days.

## Detail
Tacitus says the veteran colony had no walls. Its settlers had taken land from the Trinovantes, and the temple to the deified Claudius stood as a symbol of Roman rule. The governor was away on Mona, the procurator sent only a small force, and the rebels took the town. The defenders gathered in the temple, which fell after two days, and Tacitus adds that a detachment of the Ninth Legion sent to help was routed. Dio's account covers the same events more briefly.

Across Colchester, excavators find a layer of burnt daub, charred timber, melted glass and scorched pottery, in places up to a metre thick. Pottery and coins date it to the early 60s, which fits both AD 60 and AD 61. The podium of the Temple of Claudius survives beneath Colchester Castle. The atlas follows the common view that the revolt began in AD 60 and marks the date as approximate.

## Significance
Here the ancient account and the archaeology agree closely: a named town, a written description of its destruction, and a burnt layer to match. The sack shows how deep the resentment of the colony and its temple ran.
