## Summary
The Pillar of Eliseg, near Llangollen, is the broken shaft of a 9th-century stone cross set up by Concenn (Cyngen), king of Powys, to honour his great-grandfather Eliseg. Its long Latin inscription is known mainly from a copy made in 1696.

## Detail
The shaft stands about 2.4 m high in its original base on top of a mound, near the later Cistercian abbey of Valle Crucis, which takes its name, the valley of the cross, from it. The cross-head is lost and the lower part of the shaft is missing. When Edward Lhuyd visited in 1696 he read much of an inscription of about thirty lines, which named Concenn as the son of Cadell, grandson of Brochfael and great-grandson of Eliseg, praised Eliseg for winning back Powys from the English, and traced the dynasty back to Vortigern and the Roman emperor Maximus. Little of the text can be read now, so Lhuyd's transcript is the main witness.

The date comes from Concenn's reign: his father died in 808 by the Welsh annals, and he himself died at Rome in 854 or 855. Project Eliseg's excavations of 2010–12 (merged into this row) showed that the mound is an Early Bronze Age cairn with cist burials, deliberately reused as the cross's setting, and the site may have served as an assembly place.

## Significance
The pillar is one of the longest inscriptions from early medieval Wales and a rare statement by a Welsh king about his own family and land. Its genealogy shows how 9th-century rulers used the Roman and post-Roman past to claim authority.
