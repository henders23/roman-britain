## Summary
On 25 September 1066 Harold II surprised the Norwegian army of Harald Hardrada and Tostig at Stamford Bridge near York. Both leaders were killed.

## Detail
Hardrada, joined by Harold's exiled brother Tostig, landed on the Humber, defeated the northern earls at Fulford on 20 September and received the submission of York. Harold marched from the south with great speed and fell on the Norwegians at the crossing of the Derwent. Chronicle MSS C, D and E report the battle, with C giving most detail; the story of a lone Norwegian holding the bridge appears in a later addition to C and is best treated as tradition. Harold's victory was decisive, and the survivors left in a small part of their fleet. The Historic England register places the battlefield east of the village, but where the fighting took place is reconstructed from later accounts, and no battle archaeology has confirmed it.

## Significance
Stamford Bridge ended the last major Scandinavian bid for the English throne. It also drew Harold north just as William was waiting to cross the Channel.
