## Summary
In 995 Bishop Aldhun moved St Cuthbert's body and the bishop's seat from Chester-le-Street to Durham, after a brief stay at Ripon.

## Detail
Symeon of Durham's Libellus de exordio, written in the early 12th century, opens its third book with the move: fearing another Viking attack, Aldhun took the relics to Ripon, and on the way back the community settled on the rocky peninsula in a loop of the River Wear. A temporary church was built, and a stone church, often called the White Church, was dedicated in 998. That building was later replaced by the present cathedral, begun in 1093; the exact position of the earlier church is not established. Symeon also reports that the cart carrying the saint's body could not be moved until the community fasted and received a revelation, which is a miracle story. The better-known tale of the Dun Cow, which says a milkmaid looking for her cow led the monks to Durham, first appears centuries later and is excluded here as legend.

## Significance
The move founded Durham as the seat of the bishops and of Cuthbert's cult, which became one of the richest in England. It ends the community's journey that began on Lindisfarne.
