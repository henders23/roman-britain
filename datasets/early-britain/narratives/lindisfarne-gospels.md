## Summary
The Lindisfarne Gospels is a Latin gospel book with elaborate painted decoration, made in Northumbria in the early 8th century and now in the British Library.

## Detail
The book survives almost complete and is kept as Cotton MS Nero D IV. It contains the four gospels with portraits of the evangelists, full-page cross designs known as carpet pages and large decorated opening letters, mixing Mediterranean, Irish and Anglo-Saxon styles. In the 10th century a priest named Aldred added a word-by-word Old English translation between the lines, the earliest surviving English version of the gospels, and a note saying the book was written by Eadfrith, bishop of Lindisfarne, and bound and adorned by others. That note, written about 250 years later, is the main evidence for where and by whom the book was made. Most scholars accept it and date the work to about 710–725 from the handwriting, the decoration and Eadfrith's time as bishop, but the note is not contemporary, and some have questioned parts of it. The pin marks the priory on Holy Island, not a known workshop.

## Significance
The book shows the wealth and learning of a Northumbrian monastery associated with the cult of St Cuthbert. Its later English gloss also makes it an important early witness to the English language.
