## Summary
In about 842 or 843 Cináed mac Ailpín, known in English as Kenneth MacAlpin, became king of the Picts. He reigned until his death at Forteviot in Strathearn in 858, and later Scottish kings traced their line back to him.

## Detail
The contemporary record is thin. The Annals of Ulster report his death in 858 and call him king of the Picts, the same title they give his predecessors. The Chronicle of the Kings of Alba, put together around the late 10th or early 11th century and surviving only in the later medieval Poppleton manuscript, begins its list of kings with him. It says he ruled Pictavia for sixteen years, had ruled Dál Riata for two years before that, raided Northumbria and suffered Danish attacks, and died of a tumour in the palace of Forteviot shortly before the Ides of February. Counting back sixteen years from 858 puts his accession in 842 or 843. No source says where he became king. The Pictish kingdom had been shaken in 839, when the Annals of Ulster record a great defeat by the Vikings in which the king of Fortriu, Eóganán son of Óengus, and many others died, and several rivals seem to have competed for power afterwards. From the 12th century Scottish writers told the story as a Scottish conquest of the Picts. Modern historians, notably Alex Woolf and Dauvit Broun, point out that Cináed and his heirs were called kings of the Picts for the rest of the century, and that a Gaelic kingdom of Alba appears only around 900, when the Annals of Ulster first call his grandson Domnall king of Alba. The map uses a halo over southern Pictland, centred on Forteviot, because Forteviot is where he died, not a proven place of accession. Excavations there by the SERF project have found an early medieval royal centre and Pictish cemetery, but the palace itself has not been located.

## Significance
Cináed founded the dynasty that ruled Alba, and later Scotland, for two centuries, and from the later Middle Ages he was remembered as the first king of Scots. Recent historians see the change from Pictland to Alba as gradual, not achieved by him alone. His accession is now a symbolic starting point for that change, not its cause.
