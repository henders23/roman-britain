## Summary
In 383 the Roman army in Britain proclaimed its commander, Magnus Maximus, emperor. He crossed to Gaul, where the western emperor Gratian was killed, and ruled the western provinces until Theodosius I defeated him and had him executed in 388.

## Detail
The usurpation is reported by several writers who were close to it in time but far from Britain. Zosimus, a Greek historian writing about a century later from earlier accounts, says Maximus resented the promotion of others and stirred up the soldiers in Britain; Orosius, writing in 417–18, and Sulpicius Severus, who knew Martin of Tours and describes him at Maximus's court at Trier, say instead that the troops forced the purple on him. Prosper of Aquitaine's chronicle also records the usurpation, under 384. All agree that he took troops across the Channel, that Gratian's army deserted near Paris, and that Gratian was killed at Lyon in August 383. Maximus then held Britain, Gaul and Spain from Trier, was recognised for a time by Theodosius, invaded Italy in 387 and was defeated by Theodosius in 388 and put to death at Aquileia. No source says where in Britain he was proclaimed, so the map shows a halo over the province rather than a point. Gildas, writing in the sixth century, makes Maximus the man who stripped Britain of its soldiers and its young men, who never returned (De excidio 13–14); modern historians regard this as an exaggeration, since Roman government and some garrisons continued for another generation. Much later Welsh tradition claimed Maximus as an ancestor: the 9th-century inscription on Eliseg's Pillar traces the kings of Powys to him, and the medieval tale of the Dream of Macsen Wledig makes him a Welsh hero. That is later tradition, not evidence for the events of 383.

## Significance
Maximus was one of several usurpers launched from Britain, and his campaign took soldiers off the island at a time when its defences were already under pressure. Gildas and later writers treated his departure as the beginning of Britain's ruin; historians now see it as one step in a longer withdrawal, which is an interpretation of thin evidence.
