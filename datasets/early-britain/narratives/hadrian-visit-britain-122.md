## Summary
In 122 the emperor Hadrian came to Britain on his tour of the western provinces. His biography links the visit to the building of a frontier wall across the north.

## Detail
The main written source is the Life of Hadrian in the Historia Augusta, a late 4th-century collection of imperial biographies. It says that after reforming the army in Germany Hadrian went to Britain, put right many abuses there and was the first to build a wall, eighty miles long, to separate the Romans from the barbarians. It gives no route, no places and no length of stay. The year comes from a military diploma issued in Britain on 17 July 122, which shows that Aulus Platorius Nepos, Hadrian's friend and the governor who built much of the Wall, had only just taken over from Pompeius Falco; he probably arrived with the emperor. Fragments of a monumental Latin inscription built into St Paul's church at Jarrow (RIB 1051), naming Hadrian and the province of Britain, are often read as part of a monument celebrating the Wall, but they do not mention the visit. Because no place is recorded, the atlas draws a halo over the north rather than a point; the Wall itself is a line and is listed separately as excluded.

## Significance
The visit is the moment the province's northern frontier was fixed in stone, and it opens the atlas's high Roman phase. It also shows how thin the narrative record is: one sentence in a late biography, dated by a single diploma.
