## Summary
At Pentecost, 11 May 973, Edgar, who had been king since 959, was consecrated king at Bath by Archbishops Dunstan and Oswald. Soon afterwards he took his fleet to Chester, where other rulers of Britain pledged to be his allies.

## Detail
The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle marks the consecration under 973 with a short poem, saying that Edgar was hallowed king at Bath at Pentecost; Pentecost fell on 11 May in 973. A much fuller description comes from Byrhtferth of Ramsey's Life of St Oswald, written within about thirty years, which describes the ceremony at length; Queen Ælfthryth was also anointed, an honour few earlier English queens had received. The order of service, associated with Dunstan, is the ancestor of the rite used at later English coronations. The ceremony took place in the minster church at Bath; its building has not been found, but it probably lay at least partly beneath the present abbey, and recent excavations immediately to the south have uncovered early apsed structures. The map therefore puts a pin at the abbey with an approximate location. Why Edgar waited fourteen years to be consecrated is debated: suggestions include a link with his age or a wish to proclaim a new, imperial style of kingship. The Chronicle (versions D and E) adds that he then sailed to Chester, where six kings met him and pledged to be his allies; the twelfth-century story that eight kings rowed him on the Dee is a later embellishment and is excluded. Numismatists date a thorough reform of the coinage, introducing a single design for all mints, to about 973 or a little later.

## Significance
The consecration at Bath, the meeting at Chester and the coinage reform together display a kingdom governed from the centre and claiming supremacy over the whole of Britain. The coronation rite drawn up for the occasion shaped English coronations ever afterwards. Some historians see Edgar's reign as the time when the administrative kingdom that William the Conqueror inherited was shaped, which is an interpretation of the wider evidence.
