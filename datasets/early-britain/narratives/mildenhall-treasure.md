## Summary
A group of some 34 pieces of fine 4th-century silver tableware, led by the Great Dish, was reported as ploughed up near Mildenhall in 1942.

## Detail
According to the accepted account, Gordon Butcher struck the silver while ploughing at West Row in January 1942 and dug it out with his employer, Sydney Ford, who kept it at home. It was reported only in 1946, when a visitor recognised it; an inquest declared it treasure trove, and it passed to the British Museum (1946,1007). The hoard includes the Great Dish, over 8 kg in weight, with a central mask of Oceanus and a Bacchic procession, together with platters, bowls, goblets and spoons, a few bearing chi-rho marks. It is dated by style to the 4th century. Because there was no record of the findspot at the time, and the four-year delay led to rumours, the place and circumstances of discovery have been questioned; Richard Hobbs examined the archive in 2008 and published the treasure fully in 2016. Who owned the silver and when it was buried are not known.

## Significance
The Mildenhall silver is among the finest late Roman tableware known, and shows the wealth some owners in Britain commanded in the 4th century. Its uncertain find-story is a lesson in why recording findspots matters.
