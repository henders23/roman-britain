## Summary
Near Chichester, excavation revealed a vast courtyard residence with mosaics, painted walls and a formal garden, built in the later 1st century.

## Detail
Barry Cunliffe excavated Fishbourne between 1961 and 1969 after the site was found during trenching for a water main, and published the site and finds in two volumes in 1971. The sequence begins with military and timber buildings from the time of the conquest, followed by an early masonry house and then the great four-winged building around a garden, which Cunliffe dated to the 70s from stratified finds. Some of the earliest mosaics in Britain survive there, and the garden beds were traced from their bedding trenches. The building's scale has made its owner a long-running question. Cunliffe suggested the client king Togidubnus, known from an inscription at Chichester; Miles Russell has argued for a later date and a Roman official such as the governor Sallustius Lucullus. Neither proposal is proven, and the pack records only that the owner is debated. The date window here follows the excavator.

## Significance
Fishbourne shows how quickly Mediterranean styles of building and living reached southern Britain after the conquest. It is the largest Roman residence known north of the Alps.
