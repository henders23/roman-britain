## Summary
In about 883 the community of St Cuthbert settled at Chester-le-Street, within a former Roman fort, with the backing of the Viking king Guthred. The bishop's seat remained there until 995.

## Detail
The Historia de Sancto Cuthberto records that Abbot Eadred, guided by the saint in a vision, had the young Guthred made king and that Guthred granted the community land; Symeon's 12th-century Libellus places the settlement at Chester-le-Street in 883. The community's church stood inside the Roman fort of Concangis, and the present church of St Mary and St Cuthbert is taken to occupy its site. Several important manuscripts were written or glossed there during the community's stay, including Aldred's Old English gloss added to the Lindisfarne Gospels in the 10th century. The date and the story of the vision come from sources produced by the community itself, which had every reason to stress royal support. They are taken as good evidence for the move but not for its details.

## Significance
Chester-le-Street shows a Christian community reaching an arrangement with a Scandinavian ruler rather than simply fleeing. For just over a century it was the centre of the bishopric north of the Tees.
