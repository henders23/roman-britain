## Summary
In 841 the Annals of Ulster record a Viking longphort, or ship-camp, at Duiblinn on the Liffey. From it the Vikings raided Leinster and the midlands. The base became a permanent Scandinavian settlement and grew into the town of Dublin.

## Detail
Viking raids on Ireland are first recorded in 795, when the Annals of Ulster note the burning of Rechru (Lambay or Rathlin). For the next forty years the raiders came and went with the seasons. In 841 the annals report two longphorts, one at Linn Duachaill (Annagassan, Co. Louth) and one at Duiblinn. They say the Laigin and the Uí Néill were plundered from Duiblinn, both their lands and their churches, as far as Sliab Bladma (the Slieve Bloom mountains), and in 842 that the heathens were still at Duiblinn. This marks the change from summer raids to camps that were kept through the winter. The annals were kept by contemporary clerics, though the surviving manuscript is late medieval, and the year is secure. The site of the camp is not. Duiblinn, the 'black pool', was a tidal pool where the Poddle met the Liffey, just south of where Dublin Castle now stands. Many scholars place the longphort near there. Others prefer Kilmainham and Islandbridge, about 3 km upstream, where the largest group of Viking-age graves outside Scandinavia was found between the late 18th century and 1934. In 2003 Linzi Simpson excavated burials of four men at South Great George's Street near the black pool, and some of their radiocarbon dates run earlier than 841. That suggests a Viking presence there by the mid-9th century, if not before. The settlement was abandoned or lost after the Irish expelled the Vikings in 902 and re-established in 917. ## Significance
Dublin became the main Scandinavian town in Ireland and a centre of trade, including the slave trade, and its kings were active around the Irish Sea, in northern Britain and at York. Historians generally treat 841 as the start of permanent Viking settlement in Ireland and of Dublin as a town, although the urban character of the first base is an interpretation and is debated.
