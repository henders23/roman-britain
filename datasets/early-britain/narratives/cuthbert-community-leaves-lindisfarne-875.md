## Summary
According to the community's own histories, Bishop Eardwulf and Abbot Eadred left Lindisfarne in 875 with St Cuthbert's body as Halfdan's army moved north.

## Detail
The story is told in the Historia de Sancto Cuthberto, compiled at Chester-le-Street and Durham between the 10th and 11th centuries, and more fully in the early 12th-century Libellus de exordio attributed to Symeon of Durham. Both describe the community carrying the saint's body away from the island and moving about Northumbria for some years, including a failed attempt to sail to Ireland, before settling at Chester-le-Street. These are later, partisan accounts written to support the community's claims to land and status. Historians have pointed out that the community already had an important church at Norham on the Tweed, and some suggest its centre had shifted there before 875, so a single dramatic departure may be a later shaping of a more gradual change. The seven years of wandering and the miracles attached to it are treated here as tradition. The date 875 comes from the 12th-century Durham chronology.

## Significance
The departure marks the end of Lindisfarne as the bishop's seat and begins the history that led to Durham. It is also a good example of how a religious community remembered and retold its past.
