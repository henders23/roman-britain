## Summary
Septimius Severus spent his last three years campaigning in northern Britain and died at York on 4 February 211, the first emperor to die in the province.

## Detail
Cassius Dio, a senator who knew Severus, and Herodian, writing a generation later, describe how the ageing emperor came to Britain in 208 with his sons Caracalla and Geta and his court. Dio says the campaigns against the Caledonians and the Maeatae were hard going through forest and bog, with heavy losses and no pitched battle, and that a peace was made which the Britons soon broke, prompting a further campaign led by Caracalla. Herodian adds that Severus died of illness, and that his sons quarrelled and took his ashes to Rome. The Historia Augusta and Eutropius name Eboracum (York) as the place of death. The day is usually given as 4 February, calculated from Dio's figures for his life and reign. On the ground, a line of large marching camps across eastern Scotland, a new legionary base at Carpow on the Tay and granaries at South Shields are assigned to these wars, though dating individual camps is debated. No building in York has been identified as the imperial residence.

## Significance
For three years York was effectively the capital of the empire, and the campaigns were the last serious Roman attempt to conquer Scotland. Severus's death in Britain also triggered the struggle between his sons that ended with Geta's murder.
