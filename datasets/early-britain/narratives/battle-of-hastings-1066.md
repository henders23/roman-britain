## Summary
On 14 October 1066 William of Normandy defeated Harold II in a day-long battle on the ridge at what is now Battle, East Sussex. Harold was killed, and the atlas's timeline ends here.

## Detail
The battle is described in Chronicle MSS D and E, by William of Poitiers and in the Bayeux Tapestry, with further accounts by later writers. MS D dates it to the feast of Pope Calixtus. The English formed a shield wall on the ridge; the Normans attacked with archers, infantry and cavalry through the day, and Harold's brothers Gyrth and Leofwine also died. How Harold died is uncertain: the Tapestry's image of a figure with an arrow near the eye has been read in different ways, and later stories should not be treated as evidence. William founded Battle Abbey on the site, with the high altar said to mark where Harold fell; this foundation tradition is the main evidence for the location. No battle archaeology has been found there, and alternatives such as Caldbec Hill and Crowhurst have been proposed.

## Significance
Hastings led to William's coronation and the Norman Conquest. For this atlas it marks the end of the timeline.
