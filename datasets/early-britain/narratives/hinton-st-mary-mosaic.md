## Summary
A 4th-century mosaic floor from a villa building in Dorset shows, at its centre, a man's head and shoulders before a chi-rho monogram.

## Detail
The mosaic was found in 1963, excavated, and lifted to the British Museum in 1965. It covered two connected rooms. The smaller shows Bellerophon on Pegasus killing the Chimaera, with hunting scenes; the larger has a central roundel with a clean-shaven male bust set before a chi-rho flanked by pomegranates, surrounded by hunting scenes, a tree and four corner busts. Its style links it with a workshop based at or near Durnovaria (Dorchester), and it is dated on style to the 4th century. The central figure is widely identified as Christ, which would make it one of the earliest images of him to survive. Others have suggested an emperor, since the chi-rho was also used on imperial coinage. How far Christian and pagan themes were meant to be read together here is also debated. Renewed fieldwork by the British Museum has reinvestigated the building from which the floor came.

## Significance
If the figure is Christ, the floor is one of the earliest pictures of him anywhere. Either way, it shows Christian symbols in the home of a wealthy Romano-British family.
