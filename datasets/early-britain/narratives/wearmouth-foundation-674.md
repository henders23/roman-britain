## Summary
In 674 Benedict Biscop founded a monastery dedicated to St Peter at the mouth of the Wear on land given by King Ecgfrith, and built its church in stone with craftsmen from Gaul.

## Detail
Bede, who entered the monastery as a child, tells its story in his Lives of the Abbots: Biscop, back from his journeys to Rome, received land from Ecgfrith, brought masons from Gaul to build a church in the Roman manner and glaziers to make its windows, and filled it with books, relics and pictures from Rome. The date rests on Bede's narrative. The west wall and porch of the present St Peter's church survive from this first church, and excavations by Rosemary Cramp in the 1960s and 1970s uncovered parts of the monastic buildings, including fragments of coloured window glass, alongside the church; the below-ground remains are scheduled. Wearmouth and its twin house at Jarrow, founded a few years later (see jarrow-dedication-685), were run as one monastery on two sites. This row and the Jarrow row are kept separate because the two houses stand some 10 km apart and are dated by different kinds of evidence: Wearmouth by Bede's narrative, Jarrow by a dated inscription.

## Significance
Wearmouth brought Continental stone building, glass and a large library to Northumbria. Its library, shared with Jarrow, made possible the scholarship of Bede.
