## Summary
Bede names Rendlesham, near the River Deben in Suffolk, as a royal settlement of the East Anglian kings, and fieldwork since 2008 has found a large and wealthy early medieval site there.

## Detail
Bede records that Cedd baptised Swithhelm, king of the East Saxons, at Rendlesham, with King Æthelwold of East Anglia standing as his sponsor; that places the event within Æthelwold's reign, 655–664. For centuries the site was known only from that passage. From 2008 a project run by Suffolk County Council's archaeology service, with metal detectorists, geophysics and aerial photographs, surveyed about 170 hectares and found Anglo-Saxon activity spread over about 50 hectares: burials, workshops, imported goods, many coins and the plan of a great hall. Excavations in 2021–23 led by Christopher Scull and colleagues added detail, and a monograph was published by the Society of Antiquaries of London in 2024. The finds show occupation from the 5th to the 8th century, much longer than the single event Bede describes. That this is the royal centre Bede meant is the prevailing view, based on the name and the unusual wealth of the site, and is recorded here as an interpretation.

## Significance
Rendlesham gives the East Anglian kings a place to live as well as a place to be buried, about 6 km from Sutton Hoo. It shows how a royal centre drew in goods, craft work and food.
