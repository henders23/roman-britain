## Summary
In 731 Bede, a monk of the twin monastery of Wearmouth–Jarrow, finished his Ecclesiastical History of the English People, the main written source for the conversion period.

## Detail
Bede says so himself in the last chapter, where he summarises the history year by year, notes that he entered the monastery at the age of seven, and lists his many works. He spent almost his whole life at Wearmouth–Jarrow and lived at the Jarrow house, whose church of St Paul, dedicated in 685, still stands in part. He does not say where he wrote the history, so the pin at Jarrow is a reasonable placing, not a recorded one. Bede collected material from Canterbury, from other churches and from written records, and he usually says where it came from, but he wrote as a churchman with a clear view of what mattered. Two copies made within a few years of his death in 735 survive, the Moore Bede in Cambridge and the St Petersburg Bede, which is why the text is so well established.

## Significance
Most of what is known about the kings, bishops and conversions of the 7th century comes from this book, including several events in this atlas. Its use of dates counted from the birth of Christ also helped spread that system of dating.
