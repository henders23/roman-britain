## Summary
An inscription in St Paul's church, Jarrow, records that the church was dedicated on 23 April 685, a few years after Benedict Biscop and Ceolfrith founded the monastery there.

## Detail
Bede's Lives of the Abbots says that King Ecgfrith, pleased with Wearmouth, gave more land so that a second house could be built at Jarrow on the Tyne, and that Biscop sent Ceolfrith there with a group of monks; the foundation is usually placed in 681 or 682. The dedication itself is dated by a stone now set above the chancel arch of the church. Its Latin, cut in Roman capitals, dates the dedication of the basilica of St Paul to the ninth day before the Kalends of May in the fifteenth year of King Ecgfrith and the fourth of Abbot Ceolfrith, which works out as 23 April 685; it is catalogued as Jarrow 17 in the Corpus of Anglo-Saxon Stone Sculpture and was studied in detail by John Higgitt. The chancel of the present church is the smaller of the original Anglo-Saxon churches on the site. Excavations by Rosemary Cramp found stone monastic buildings and window glass to its south. The founding of Wearmouth is a separate row (wearmouth-foundation-674).

## Significance
The stone is one of the earliest closely dated inscriptions in England and fixes the beginning of the house where Bede lived and wrote. It is a rare case of a day-precise date for a 7th-century English building.
