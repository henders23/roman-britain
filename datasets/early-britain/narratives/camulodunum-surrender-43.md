## Summary
In AD 43 the emperor Claudius came to Britain, captured Camulodunum and received the submission of British rulers. His triumphal arch in Rome later claimed that eleven kings had surrendered to him.

## Detail
According to Dio, the army halted at the Thames and waited for Claudius, who came with reinforcements, crossed the river, defeated the Britons and took Camulodunum, the stronghold of Cunobelinus's successors. He won over a number of tribes, some by agreement and some by force, and then returned to Rome after only a few days in Britain. The arch the Senate voted him, completed in the early 50s, carried an inscription (CIL VI 920). In its usual restoration it says he received the surrender of eleven British kings without loss and was the first to bring peoples beyond the Ocean under Roman rule.

The inscription survives only as fragments, and the numeral is partly restored. A. A. Barrett has argued that the wording points to diplomatic submissions rather than battlefield victories. Neither source says the kings submitted at Camulodunum itself, so the pin marks Colchester in general. The pre-Roman centre was a spread-out complex of dykes and settlements to the west of the later Roman town.

## Significance
The capture of Camulodunum made Claudius's personal share in the conquest complete, and he celebrated it lavishly at Rome. Camulodunum then became the first Roman colony and provincial centre in Britain, which later made it the rebels' first target in the revolt.
