## Summary
Excavations on the Bloomberg site beside the Walbrook in the City of London produced over 400 wooden writing tablets from the first decades of Roman London, among them a loan note dated 8 January AD 57, the earliest dated handwritten document from Britain.

## Detail
MOLA excavated the site between 2010 and 2014, ahead of Bloomberg's European headquarters. Waterlogged ground along the Walbrook stream preserved timber buildings and thousands of objects, including about 405 stylus tablets. These were thin wooden boards once coated with wax; the wax has perished, but where the stylus cut through it into the wood, the text can sometimes be recovered. Roger Tomlin read 87 of them, published in 2016 as MOLA Monograph 72. The tablets come from deposits dated to roughly AD 50–80, and some belong to the town's earliest years, before the revolt of AD 60.

Tablet WT44 records that a man named Tibullus owed 105 denarii to Gratus, with the date given by the consuls as 8 January AD 57. Tomlin reads both men as freed slaves, probably acting for their former owners, though this is an interpretation. Other tablets include contracts, accounts and an address to a man named Mogontius in London, the earliest written use of the town's name.

## Significance
The tablets show Londinium as a commercial town from its first years, with money lending, contracts and letters in everyday use. They give names and voices to people otherwise absent from the record.
