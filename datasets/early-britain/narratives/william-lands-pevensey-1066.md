## Summary
On 28 September 1066 Duke William's fleet landed at Pevensey, where the Normans occupied the Roman fort before moving to Hastings.

## Detail
The Chronicle (MS D) says William came to Pevensey on Michaelmas Eve and, once ready, built a castle at Hastings. William of Poitiers, the duke's chaplain, describes the crossing and landing, and the Bayeux Tapestry shows the fleet arriving with a titulus naming Pevensey, then horses being unloaded and the army hurrying to Hastings. In 1066 Pevensey stood beside a tidal lagoon and the coastline has since moved, so the landing beach cannot be located; the pin marks the late Roman fort of Anderitum, whose walls the Normans used for their first defences. The landing was unopposed because Harold's army was in Yorkshire. The account of William stumbling as he came ashore is later tradition, not recorded in the contemporary sources.

## Significance
The landing began the Norman Conquest. Its lack of opposition, caused by events in the north, gave William time to establish himself before Harold returned.
