## Summary
Inchtuthil, on a plateau above the Tay in Perthshire, is a legionary fortress begun in the early 80s AD and taken down, unfinished, a few years later. Because nothing was built over it, it gives the only complete plan of a legionary fortress known in the Roman Empire.

## Detail
Excavations directed by Ian Richmond and J. K. St Joseph between 1952 and 1965, published by Lynn Pitts and St Joseph in 1985, recovered the plan of the 21.5-hectare fortress from its timber foundation trenches: barracks, granaries, a hospital, a workshop and a small headquarters building, but no commander's house, which had not yet been built. The fortress was then dismantled in an orderly way. Timbers were removed, pottery and glass smashed, and about seven tonnes of iron nails buried in a pit in the workshop so they could not be reused by enemies.

The start date comes from Agricola's campaigns, so about 82–83. The end rests on coins: the latest are bronze issues of Domitian of AD 86 in almost unworn condition, found here and at other Scottish forts, which points to abandonment in late 86 or 87. The withdrawal is usually linked to the transfer of a legion from Britain to the Danube. The garrison is generally taken to be Legio XX, and the name Pinnata Castra has been proposed, but neither is attested on the site.

## Significance
Inchtuthil shows Rome planning to hold Scotland permanently, and then changing its mind within a few years. Its untouched plan is a reference point for understanding every other legionary fortress.
