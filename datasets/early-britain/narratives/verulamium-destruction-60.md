## Summary
Verulamium (St Albans) was the third town the rebels destroyed. Excavation found shops on Watling Street that had burnt down at about this time.

## Detail
Tacitus names Verulamium, a town of the Catuvellauni, together with London as places the rebels destroyed. Otherwise he says little about it, so most of what is known of the destruction comes from excavation.

Between 1957 and 1961 Sheppard Frere excavated a range of timber shops on the Watling Street frontage in Insula XIV. They had been burnt around AD 60, apparently set alight from the street side, and the burnt layer was made up of clean red daub and ash. Frere's report maps where Boudican fire deposits were found in the town. They are patchy, and some buildings nearby survived, so how completely the town was destroyed is still debated. The atlas follows the common view that the revolt began in AD 60 and marks the date as approximate.

## Significance
Verulamium shows that the rebels attacked a town of Britons friendly to Rome as well as Roman colonists and traders. It is also a useful case of archaeology testing how complete a written account of destruction really was.
