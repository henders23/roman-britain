## Summary
In the winter of 873–4 the Viking 'great army' camped at Repton, a Mercian royal and religious centre, and drove King Burgred out of Mercia. Excavation has identified the camp beside St Wystan's church.

## Detail
The Chronicle records that the army moved from Lindsey to Repton, took winter quarters there, drove Burgred overseas and put Ceolwulf in his place. Excavations by Martin Biddle and Birthe Kjølbye-Biddle in the 1970s and 1980s (a merged row) found a large ditch that used the church as part of a D-shaped enclosure open to the Trent, burials with Scandinavian weapons and ornaments, and a grave holding five coins struck around 872–5. Nearby, a stone building reused as a mortuary chamber held the disarticulated bones of at least 264 people. The charnel's link to the army was long doubted because earlier radiocarbon dates came out too old; a 2018 study correcting for the marine diet of the dead brought them into line with the 870s. That the enclosure, the burials and the charnel all belong to the army is interpretation, strengthened but not proved by the dating.

## Significance
Repton is one of the few places where a Viking army's documented movements can be matched on the ground. It shows how the army used an existing royal and church site as a base.
