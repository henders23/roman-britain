## Summary
Suetonius Paulinus defeated the rebel army in a pitched battle, and soon afterwards Boudica died. Neither ancient account says where the battle was fought.

## Detail
Tacitus says Paulinus had perhaps 10,000 men. He chose a position in a narrow valley with woodland behind, so that he could only be attacked from the front, and there he met a much larger British force that had brought its families in wagons to watch. After Boudica spoke to her army, the Romans held their ground, then drove forward, and the Britons were trapped against their own wagons. Tacitus says Boudica took poison. Dio, who gives a longer and more rhetorical account, says she fell ill and died.

The site is unknown. Because Paulinus was coming from the north-west, most historians place it on or near Watling Street in the Midlands. Mancetter in Warwickshire is the site most often proposed; High Cross and Church Stowe are others, and some writers look much further afield. No battlefield archaeology has been confirmed. The atlas therefore draws a broad halo and gives no pin. It follows the common view that the revolt began in AD 60 and marks the date as approximate.

## Significance
The battle ended the most serious rising Rome faced in Britain. Its lost location is a warning against turning traditional identifications into points on a map.
