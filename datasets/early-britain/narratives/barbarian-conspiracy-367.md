## Summary
In 367 Britain was hit by attacks from the Picts, Scots and Attacotti while Franks and Saxons raided Gaul. The historian Ammianus Marcellinus calls it a barbarian conspiracy.

## Detail
Ammianus reports that the emperor Valentinian, on the way to Trier, heard that a barbarian conspiracy had reduced Britain to desperate straits: Nectaridus, count of the coastal region, had been killed and the general Fullofaudes ambushed. Deserters and the areani, scouts who were meant to watch the peoples beyond the frontier, had joined in. After two commanders were sent and recalled, Count Theodosius crossed from Boulogne, attacked raiding bands near London, offered pardons to deserters, and then restored cities and forts, disbanded the areani and recovered a province renamed Valentia. Tomlin argues that this all happened between 367 and the end of 368. How co-ordinated the attacks were is debated. Ammianus wrote some twenty years later, in the reign of Theodosius I, the count's son, and gave the father unusual space and praise. Many historians therefore suspect that separate raids meeting a weak garrison were magnified into a single plot. Burnt layers once dated to 367 at sites across Britain are now mostly not accepted as evidence.

## Significance
The events of 367 are the fullest account of a military crisis in late Roman Britain, and they name the peoples who would raid the island over the next century. They also show how far the history of the province depends on one historian with his own reasons for telling the story as he did.
