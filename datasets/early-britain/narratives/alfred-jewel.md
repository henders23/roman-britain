## Summary
A gold, enamel and rock-crystal ornament inscribed 'Alfred ordered me to be made', found in 1693 a few miles from Athelney and now in the Ashmolean Museum.

## Detail
The jewel was ploughed up in 1693 at Newton Park in the parish of North Petherton, on land of Sir Thomas Wroth; William Musgrave published it in the Philosophical Transactions in 1698, and Colonel Nathaniel Palmer gave it to Oxford, where it entered the Ashmolean in 1718. A slice of rock crystal covers a cloisonné enamel half-figure, perhaps a personification of sight or Christ, set in a gold frame with an Old English inscription in openwork letters and ending in an animal head holding a small socket. The inscription names an Ælfred but not a king. Its identification with King Alfred, and so the date window of 871–899, rests on that name together with the find-spot near Athelney and the style of the goldwork; it is widely accepted but not proved. Most scholars take the socket to have held a pointer, linking the jewel with the aestels Alfred said he sent with copies of his translation of Gregory's Pastoral Care, though other uses have been proposed. The 1693 discovery is merged into this row.

## Significance
It is the best-known object associated with Alfred and one of the finest pieces of late Anglo-Saxon goldwork. If it is an aestel, it is direct material evidence for Alfred's programme of learning.
